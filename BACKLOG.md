# Backlog

- **user_progress last-review-wins.** A restored write can overwrite newer SM-2 state from another device. Not fixed because `updated_at` is not comparable across clients: the frozen Flutter app writes local time with no timezone, so its values are off by the local offset (3h for Uruguay). The clean fix is a `reviewed_at timestamptz` column plus a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger that keeps the row with the later `reviewed_at`. Narrow window: needs an app kill with unsent ratings AND use of another device before the next open.
- Flutter wrote `updated_at` as a local time without timezone on `user_progress` and `user_settings`, so those stored values are shifted. Only matters if we ever compare timestamps across clients.
- An English-only user is still gated on Russian text: `hasTranslations` (src/data/words.ts) requires both an English and a Russian translation for a word to enter Learn, Review and practice, whatever the translation switches say. Left alone on purpose when the switches were made to govern what is shown: changing it changes which words are learnable (and the pool sizes, the daily counts and the Matching and Cloze eligibility with it), which is a separate decision. Today every dictionary word has both, so it only bites for custom words with one translation.
- **Re-adding a deleted custom word revives its old progress.** Deleting a custom word removes only its `user_words` row; its `user_progress`, `user_favorites` and `user_hidden_words` rows are left, as the Flutter app leaves them. Adding the same word again (same `es_word`) therefore brings back the old repetitions, schedule, star and hidden flag. Left alone to stay compatible with the Flutter app; a clean delete would also remove those rows.
- **A deleted custom word can come back from a Flutter device that still holds it.** The Flutter app pushes every custom word it has locally on each sync and its pull never deletes a local word that is missing from the cloud, so a device that had the word before it was deleted elsewhere (here, or on another Flutter device) writes it back. The tombstone in `pending_word_deletes` only stops that app's pull from resurrecting a word it deleted itself.
- **Custom words lose their inflected word form.** `word_form_in_example` has no column in `user_words`, so the form the enrichment returns is thrown away on the web and is never synced. The web highlights (and the Cloze blanks) a custom word only where the sentence contains it as typed; an inflected form ("Tengo" for "tener") is not found, and a Rioplatense form never becomes the headword. Fixing it needs a new column on a table the Flutter app also uses.
- **High priority — the streak bar is too low.** One Review rating counts the whole day: the four qualifying events (a finished Learn batch, one Review rating, a completed Matching group, the last Cloze question) all count equally, and all count at one. Decide a real bar: a number of reviews, a share of the daily goal, or a minimum across modes. The Flutter app writes the same `streak_count` / `streak_last_activity_date` pair, so a new rule has to be one both apps can live with, or the web has to own the rule entirely (and say so, since the Flutter app would keep earning days at the old bar and the two would disagree).
- **High priority — FSRS instead of SM-2.** Anki has moved from its SM-2 variant to FSRS, a model of memory (difficulty, stability, and the chance of remembering at a given moment) fitted on a very large corpus of real reviews. It predicts forgetting better than SM-2 and shows a word fewer times for the same retention, and the algorithm and its default parameters are public. We use SM-2 (`src/sm2/sm2.ts`, a port of the Flutter app's `Word.applyReview`) only because the frozen Flutter app wrote the same `user_progress` rows; that constraint is gone. What the migration touches: `user_progress` carries `ease_factor`, `interval` and `repetitions`, which FSRS replaces with stability and difficulty (plus a last-review time and a lapse count), so it needs new columns and a rule for converting every existing row (roughly: stability from the current interval, difficulty from the ease factor) that neither resets anyone's progress nor makes a whole backlog come due at once. It also reaches everything built on SM-2's fields: the interval previews on the rating buttons, the relearning step and the state Learn leaves a word in (`repetitions 1, interval 0`), the Words screen's stages ("In progress" is `repetitions` 1, "Known well" is 2 or more), the review ordering (most overdue first could become least likely to be remembered first), the write queue's progress upsert and the restore rules, and the tests around all of it.
- **A shared pool of user-contributed words.** A word a user adds can also go to a public list that other users can take into their own vocabulary.
- **Rejected, measured: a regional preference (Argentine or Uruguayan).** The idea was a setting so a card that leads with "ómnibus (uy), also colectivo (ar)" would lead with the other form for someone who picks Argentina. The count that decides it was made on the shipped overlay (`public/rio_overlay.json`): one entry out of 76 has two regional variants, autobús (ómnibus / colectivo), the only pair that two independent families of sources agree on for both countries. One word is far too few to justify a row in Settings, so it was not built; do not reopen it from scratch. Worth revisiting only if the overlay grows several times over (a few hundred entries, so that there are dozens of such pairs). The count has fallen as the data was checked: it first read five, because foco carried an `alt_form` ("lámpara") with no country, which was one respondent's own word and not a regional variant; then four, and then one, when the country labels were put under the two-source rule and the pairs guay / copado, portero / arquero and asilo / geriátrico were found to rest on a single source (for copado and arquero the other sources say both countries use the word).
- **Method: the questionnaire only asked Uruguayans, so it cannot establish that a form is Uruguay-only.** Every question was "¿En Uruguay …?", asked of 8 Uruguayans: it shows that Uruguayans use a word, never that Argentines do not. The overlay used to set `region: 'uy'` by rule on that evidence alone, and ten entries carried a country label nobody had earned; they are now null unless two independent families of sources say the other country does not use the form (`tools/rio-overlay/regionRule.mjs`). A future round has to ask Argentines the same questions, or the data will keep carrying this asymmetry: until then a label can only come from the model runs and DAMER, never from the questionnaire.
- **Decide what goes behind premium.**
- **Real statistics.** Today the app shows a streak and seven activity dots. Worth having: how many words are known at each stage, a forecast of the coming review load (how many reviews fall due each day over the next weeks), retention over time, and the words that keep lapsing. `user_daily_metrics` already records per-day activity (new words, reviews done, reviews lapsed, how many were due at the start of the day, the learn pool and the daily limit), so some of this needs no new writes: the history of the review load, a rough retention curve (1 − lapsed / done per day) and the new-word pace. The forecast and the counts per stage come from `user_progress` as it is now. The words that keep lapsing are the exception: a lapse is counted per day, never per word, so that one needs a lapse count on the progress row (which FSRS wants as well, see the FSRS entry).
- **Pronunciation (to be discussed before building: the most expensive item here).** This entry records the question, not an answer. Recorded native audio or text-to-speech? Native audio sounds best but costs a speaker's time for every word, and a speaker whose Spanish is Rioplatense; text-to-speech is cheap and covers any word, including the ones users add, but is there a voice that is acceptable for Rioplatense (the "ll" and "y" sound, voseo forms)? What it costs: recording or generating audio for the roughly 4,700 dictionary words (and the example sentences?), and a running cost if speech is generated on demand. Where it lives: files in `public/` next to the dictionary, or a storage bucket, with the size and the offline behaviour that follow. And what it covers: every word, or only the Rioplatense overlay entries (76 today), where the pronunciation is the furthest from what a learner expects of textbook Spanish.
- **Phrases, not only single words.** The add-word form rejects anything with a space (`checkSpanish` in `src/data/customWords.ts`, inherited from the Flutter app; the proxy's `/enrich` does not). Users will ask for "estar seguro", "de una", "ni ahí". Lifting the restriction touches the duplicate check (`findDuplicate` matches a typed word against es_word and the overlay forms), the headword rule (what leads a card and what is highlighted in its example), enrichment (the prompt asks about a word, and `word_form_in_example` must be an exact substring of the example) and Cloze (what gets blanked). The overlay already has multi-word forms ("de más", "casa de salud", "con vos"), so part of this exists. It is also the honest answer to word senses: "estar seguro" as its own entry beats splitting "seguro" into meanings.
- **The reverse direction: Russian or English to Spanish.** Everything the app does today trains recognition: Learn shows a Spanish word with its meaning, and Review and Matching ask for the meaning of a Spanish word. Only Cloze asks for production, and then with a cue (the sentence around the blank). A reverse card shows the meaning and asks the user to recall the Spanish word, which is the skill people actually complain about missing. It needs a decision first: a separate mode, or reverse cards mixed into Review? And whether a reverse card carries its own SM-2 state or shares the word's: recognising a word and producing it are different memories, so sharing would schedule one by the other, while its own state means a second row per word, and `user_progress` is keyed on (user_id, es_word), so the key needs a direction. Two smaller questions come with it: a meaning is often shared by several Spanish words (and by a word's Rioplatense and standard forms), so what counts as the right answer; and how the answer is given, typed or self-rated.
- **A service worker, so the app opens offline from a cold start.** Today an offline launch needs the page and the dictionary to be loadable; the queue and the in-memory flow already work without a network once the app is open.
- **The headword highlight test is slow.** "finds a highlight for every dictionary word" (src/data/headword.test.ts) took about 5.4 s against the 5 s default under a parallel run and flaked, so it has its own 30 s timeout. Worth checking whether the highlight search itself is slow, since the app runs it for every card.
